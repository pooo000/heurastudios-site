Coming soon to Heura Studios · Report, November 24, 2026

Heura Asteria

Heura’s GPU models, in three sizes: 2B, 10B and 50B. Training moves off the CPU and onto data-center GPUs, and you pick the size each agent needs.

  • Why: Heura Arche trains and answers on our server’s CPU in 32-bit, so a small 3-round training takes about 6 hours and an answer about 90 seconds. A data-center GPU does the same math roughly 260 to 4,000 times faster at peak.
  • The sizes: 2B for fast, simple agents that can run on a laptop; 10B for most business agents; 50B for the hardest work, on data-center GPUs.
  • Same Studio: write the script, add examples and tests, train. Only the model under it changes.

Heura Arche today

Arche is a 2B model that trains and answers on a CPU. It runs anywhere, and it is slow.

Why a GPU is faster

Training and answering are almost all matrix math. A GPU runs the same code; it just does each step’s math far faster:

  • Thousands of cores at once. A model step is millions of multiply-adds that don’t depend on each other, and a GPU runs thousands of them in parallel.
  • 16-bit math. GPUs train in bfloat16, half the memory of 32-bit and far faster on their tensor cores.
  • Faster memory. Every word an answer writes reads the whole model from memory, and an H100 reads 3.35 TB a second.

Speed and costFastest, cheapestMiddleSlowest, priciest per answer

The 2B is Arche’s size on a GPU: faster, not smarter. Bigger sizes understand more. When we trained a 4B with Arche’s recipe, it beat every 2B version on our understanding, new-business-task and learn-from-one-answer tests.
